The consolidation of meaning

Thirty-five models, built by different teams on different text, agree on how their concepts sit relative to one another. Each model’s coordinates are its own; the angles between its concepts are not.

Is this the structure of language itself, or the structure of the world it describes?

About 35 seconds. What might this mean? Models built by different teams, on different text, agree on how their concepts sit relative to one another. That is what the Platonic Representation Hypothesis predicts, and because the map is measurable, we can test it concept by concept and model by model. Which is part of what interpretability is for. The open question: is this the structure of language itself, or of the world it describes? Come find the poster; the code and reading list are on GitHub. If asked about scale or theory: a recent paper (Balestriero and LeCun, 2026, arXiv:2610.39302) argues that alignment depends on how training views are chosen, not on scale alone. It is vision-language and trains new models; this work is text-only across existing language models, so it neither confirms nor contradicts it.
