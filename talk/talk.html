People say AI is a “black box”, but that is not strictly true. Mechanistic interpretability is an attempt to understand how a model represents information. You must hold the model’s weights to do it.

Concepts are vectors: the Linear Representation Hypothesis

A concept is a direction

A model stores each concept it learns, such as negation, as a vector in its activation space. The more strongly a prompt expresses the concept, the further it lands along that vector.

Park, K., Choe, Y. J., & Veitch, V. (2024). The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models. ICML.

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis

Different shadows, one world?

What converges is relations, not coordinates.

Prediction: models that never shared a weight should agree on how concepts relate to each other.

Huh, M., Cheung, B., Wang, T., & Isola, P. (2024). The Platonic Representation Hypothesis. ICML.

The question for the poster

Do independently trained models agree?

If the hypothesis is right, models built by different teams on different text should agree on how their concepts relate to one another. I tested that across 35 open models, concept by concept and layer by layer.

If they do, is it the structure of language itself, or of the world it describes?
